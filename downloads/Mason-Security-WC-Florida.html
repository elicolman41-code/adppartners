Armed guards. ADP can't write the class. Goal: place comp elsewhere, keep the payroll on ADP, avoid the PEO.

The one thing that makes this work

A workers' comp policy placed outside ADP can still bill off ADP payroll. InsurePay — the premium-billing platform most pay-as-you-go carriers use — lists ADP Workforce Now and RUN Powered by ADP among its automated payroll connections.

So the outside broker binds the policy, the carrier bills through InsurePay, and ADP supplies the payroll feed. Mason gets pay-as-you-go cash flow, stays on ADP, and the PEO conversation goes away. This is the argument.

Confirm the specific carrier uses InsurePay at quote stage — it's carrier-by-carrier. Don't promise integration before the carrier is named.

The state fund is a fallback you have to earn

The FWCJUA is Florida's market of last resort. It is not a parallel option:

  • Requires written declinations from two private insurers before it will look at a submission — so save every decline letter as it arrives.
  • Only a specifically authorized producer can file. Underwriting: 941-378-7400.
  • An offer of voluntary coverage during the first 30 days kills eligibility.
  • Whether armed guards are eligible at all is unconfirmed. One call answers it — worth making early, so you know if a floor exists.
Tell Mason this before he signs anything there

Tier 3 FWCJUA policies are assessable. If premiums don't cover losses, the policyholder can be billed more after the fact — an open-ended exposure on his balance sheet. Tier 1 runs about 1.81× manual rate, Tier 2 about 2.66×, and every tier adds a flat $475 surcharge. This is expensive money.

Two corrections worth having

Do not quote a rate yet

The figures circulating for Florida security classes — 7720 at $2.57 and 7723 at $2.48 per $100 of payroll — come from broker lead-gen sites, not NCCI or the Florida OIR. They imply armed guards cost less than unarmed, which is backwards. Have the placing agent pull the current NCCI filing, or use the FWCJUA pricing tool, before any dollar figure is spoken aloud.

FDACS licensing does not require workers' comp. Under §493.6110, a Class "B" agency license requires commercial general liability of at least $300,000 combined single limit — and letting that CGL cancel automatically suspends the agency license.

Workers' comp is driven by two other things: Chapter 440 (four or more employees, non-construction) and — far more binding in practice — his client contracts. Property managers, HOAs, hospitals and municipal contracts all demand a comp certificate regardless of headcount. If Mason thinks his license is at risk, it isn't; his contracts are.

What Mason gathers before any market sees this

Underwriters price the account mix more than the headcount. A complete file is the difference between a quote and a decline.

  1. ACORD 130 and ACORD 125, completed and signed
  2. Loss runs, 3–5 years, currently valued, from every prior carrier — including nil-loss letters. No underwriter quotes without these.
  3. Experience mod worksheet, or written confirmation he isn't rated yet
  4. Payroll split armed vs. unarmed, by class code and state — current estimate plus two years actual
  5. Employee census: title, armed or unarmed, FDACS license class (D vs. G) and expiration for each
  6. Class "B" agency license plus the Class "D" and "G" roster
  7. Certificate showing the §493.6110 CGL is in force
  8. Firearms training and requalification records — curriculum, instructor credentials, dated records per armed officer
  9. Written use-of-force policy and post orders
  10. Hiring and background-check procedure in writing; MVRs if guards drive
  11. Client contract schedule — each account, site type, armed or unarmed, hours, revenue
  12. OSHA 300A and vehicle schedule if applicable

What gets a quote: clean loss runs, mod at or under 1.00, documented firearms requalification, static retail/commercial/residential posts, written procedures, low turnover.

What gets declined: armed exposure discovered at audit rather than disclosed, armored or cash-in-transit work, executive protection, bar and nightclub posts, mod well above 1.00, gaps in coverage history.
