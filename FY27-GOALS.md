## 3. Strategy — built around the dollar instinct, not against it

**Design principle:** Eli will always take the dollar deal. Fine — dollar
deals feed the $215K side of the SDM window and the $300K quota, and deal
speed is a proven strength. So dollars need *zero* dedicated strategy.
The whole plan is making unit volume so large and so frictionless that units
flow even while dollars get chased.

Since conversion is ~100%, **units-in ≈ referrals-in.** Three unit engines,
in order of speed-to-impact:

### Engine 1 — Activate existing partners (fastest, works this month)
Devon (Dixon Consulting), Otis McQueen (O Financial Solutions), Emil.
Their books are untapped and the missing ingredient is **cadence**, not
economics (revenue share + goodwill already in place).
- Standing biweekly touch with each partner (call or coffee — relationship
  channel, plays to strength; no email drip).
- At each touch, one concrete ask: "who in your book is running payroll
  badly / hiring / complaining?" — mine the book, don't wait for referrals
  to occur to them.
- Target: lift combined referral flow measurably within Q1 (baseline gets
  established in month 1 — see §5).

### Engine 2 — The boss list (self-sourced ~25%)
The list from the manager is the main self-source channel.
- Make the ask systematic: request the list on a fixed monthly rhythm
  instead of ad hoc.
- Side benefit: every list conversation is a natural SDM-progress
  conversation with the person closest to the promotion numbers.

### Engine 3 — 5 new referring partners (slow lag — plant now, harvest winter)
Ramp time varies wildly (Devon vs. Otis took months), so partners signed in
Jul–Sep are what makes January and spring strong. **The target list already
exists in this repo** (43 ranked targets in
`output/new_partnership_targets_2026.csv`, plus outreach templates) — the
work is relationship-building, not list-building.
- Cadence: a fixed number of new-partner conversations per week (2–3),
  time-boxed so it can't cannibalize referral servicing.
- Warmest first: ask Devon/Otis/Emil for introductions before cold outreach.
- 5 signed = roughly one every 10 weeks. Front-load: aim for 2 by end of
  September so their ramp overlaps the January window.

### Resolving the draft's "strategic conflict"
The draft flagged partner acquisition vs. unit conversion as competing for
the same hours. Resolution: they are not equal priorities. **Engine 1 and
inbound referral closing always outrank Engine 3.** New-partner work gets a
fixed, capped weekly time box; it never expands into referral-servicing time.
That is the explicit time-allocation decision the draft demanded.

## 5. Operating system (built on the habit that already exists)

Eli already keeps a monthly pipeline scorecard. Don't invent a new system —
**extend the existing one with four input lines:**

1. Referrals received this month (per partner)
2. Units closed (toward rolling-12: ___/80)
3. Rolling-12 dollars ($___/215K)
4. New-partner conversations held / partners signed (___/5)

Month 1 (July) is the baseline month: the first real count of referrals-in
per partner. Every later decision (which partner to push, whether Engine 3
needs more hours) comes off these four numbers.

Anti-reactive guard: the biweekly partner touches (Engine 1) and the weekly
new-partner block (Engine 3) are the only two calendar commitments. Two
commitments is the maximum structure a reactive operator will actually keep.
Everything else stays flexible — that flexibility is what lets the
dollar-deal instinct run free without breaking the plan.
