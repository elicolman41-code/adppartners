like you, and it could add a real revenue line to MRE."

the full value of the partnership, because a lot of partners aren't."

================================================================================
MEETING AGENDA (30-minute structure)
================================================================================

MINUTES 0-5: RAPPORT + ESTABLISH CREDIBILITY
  - Acknowledge Goldman/PwC background — treat him as a peer, not a prospect
  - "I did some homework — the AI positioning is interesting. That's rare
    for a firm at your stage. How long have you been building that out?"
  - Let him talk about the firm. He's proud of it. Let him brag.

MINUTES 5-12: DISCOVERY — THE ONLY QUESTIONS THAT MATTER
  Ask these in order. Don't pitch until you have the answers.

  Q1: "When your clients come to you needing payroll, what are you
      typically recommending right now?"
      [Goal: Find out if there's a competitor or a gap]

  Q2: "Of your 180+ clients, roughly how many are businesses with
      employees on payroll — vs. solo operators or investors?"
      [Goal: Quantify the referral opportunity]

  Q3: "Are you currently earning anything on those payroll referrals —
      through Gusto, QuickBooks, or anyone else?"
      [Goal: If the answer is no or very little, that's your opening]

  Q4: "With the AI automation work you're doing — are you helping clients
      with workflow automation around HR and payroll, or is that more
      on the tax and operations side?"
      [Goal: Understand if the AI angle creates a natural ADP integration story]

MINUTES 12-22: THE PITCH — SHORT, MONEY-FORWARD
  Lead with exactly one thing: the revenue share math.

  "Here's what I want to show you, Matthew. You're already in the
  conversation with 180+ business clients. Some of them need payroll.
  Right now you're either pointing them somewhere and earning nothing,
  or not pointing them anywhere at all.

  ADP has a formal referral partner program. For every client you refer,
  you earn recurring monthly revenue — not a one-time fee, recurring.
  The pricing your clients pay is comparable to Gusto or QuickBooks.
  But what we pay you is significantly higher than what those platforms
  pay their partners.

  For a firm positioned as a one-stop solution, adding ADP to your stack
  means you actually ARE the one stop. Tax, insurance, AI, and now payroll.
  That's a cleaner story for your clients and a new revenue line for you."

  [PAUSE — let him respond]

  If he asks about numbers: "I'd want to understand your client breakdown
  a bit more before I give you a specific projection, but typically for
  a practice with 50-100 payroll referrals a year, we're talking about
  income that would surprise you. Can we schedule a follow-up where I
  put together the actual math for your volume?"

MINUTES 22-28: THE AI ANGLE (use if the conversation goes there)
  Matthew positions as AI-forward. Use it.

  "One thing that actually fits your AI brand — ADP's platform has
  automation and analytics built in that your clients will actually use.
  Real-time dashboards, automated tax filings, compliance monitoring.
  When you refer a client to ADP, you're recommending something that
  fits the 'AI-driven' story you're already telling. That's different
  from just saying 'use Gusto.'"

MINUTES 28-30: CLOSE — SET THE NEXT STEP
  Don't try to close the partnership on this call. Close the next meeting.

  "Matthew — I don't want to throw numbers at you without understanding
  your client breakdown better. What I'd like to do is a 20-minute
  follow-up where I put together a specific revenue projection based on
  your actual client volume. That way you can look at a real number and
  decide if it makes sense. Does that work?"

  OR if he's engaged: "Should I send you the partner program overview
  today and we schedule a follow-up to go through the specifics?"

RISK FLAGS — THINGS TO WATCH FOR
================================================================================

  ⚠️  Great Neck / Long Island base — not Brooklyn. Confirm his clients
      are in your territory before investing heavily in this relationship.
      Ask: "Are most of your clients Long Island-based or do you have
      a lot of NYC clients too?"

  ⚠️  Goldman/PwC background = sophisticated buyer. He will ask hard
      questions about the program structure, fees, and exclusivity.
      Know your numbers before this call.

  ⚠️  Newer firm = smaller client book than "180+" may suggest.
      Verify how many of those 180 are businesses with employees
      vs. individuals or LLCs with no payroll.

  ⚠️  Insurance consulting is a core pillar — check if he already
      has a relationship with a PEO or benefits broker that bundles
      payroll. If so, there may be a conflict. Ask early.

  ⚠️  Solo firm = no committee, which is good. But Matthew decides
      everything, so if he doesn't like the deal, there's no workaround.
      Build personal rapport, not just the business case.
