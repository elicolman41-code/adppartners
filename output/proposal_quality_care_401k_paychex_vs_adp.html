Two core differences: (1) Your Paychex administration fee is variable — it has already crept up this year, and keeping it down means calling in to renegotiate. ADP’s fee is a fixed, contractual formula: a set base plus $2.50 per participant. (2) Paychex ACHs its admin fees straight out of your business bank account — you pay them out of pocket. ADP’s fees are billed to the plan, so your out-of-pocket cost is $0.

You are paying Paychex out of pocket. Your billing statements show the retirement admin charge as “ACH FROM ACCOUNT …713” — pulled directly from your business bank account, roughly $192–$214/month ($2,424/yr). With ADP these fees are charged to the plan instead, so your business pays nothing out of pocket.
Your Paychex admin fee is moving. Your statements show the base admin fee rose from $135 → $150/mo and the per-participant fee from $5 → $6 within a few months, with extra back-billed “adjustments.” Paychex’s own disclosures confirm these rates are “set forth on your invoice” and can change. ADP’s base ($125) and per-participant ($2.50) are locked by contract.

Why this matters most: Paychex’s Fidelity Advisor M-share funds cost roughly 1% per year on a typical target-date allocation, and 25–50 bps of that is revenue-sharing routed back to Paychex. ADP’s funds run 0.07%. On your $218K balance that is a difference of roughly $2,000 every year — and it grows as the plan grows. ADP also includes a 3(38) investment manager who takes legal fiduciary responsibility for fund selection; Paychex does not.
Limited-time: 8 months free. Sign on by Friday and ADP waives 8 months of recordkeeping fees in Year 1 — a credit of about $1,795 applied to the plan. That drops your Year 1 all-in cost to roughly $1,269.

Both plans have asset-based components, but Paychex’s are higher and its admin fee is discretionary. The more your plan grows, the more ADP saves.

Assumes Paychex fund expenses at ~1.0% and the current admin fee held flat (in practice it has been rising). ADP figures use the contractual formula: $1,500 base + $2.50/participant + 0.45% + 0.10% + 0.07%.

Bottom line: Paychex looks cheaper on the admin line, but its ~1% fund expenses, embedded revenue-sharing, and a variable admin fee make it more expensive all-in — roughly $1,540 more per year today, widening as you grow. ADP is lower all-in, transparent, contractually fixed on the dollar fees, and includes a 3(38) fiduciary.
