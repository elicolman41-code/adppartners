## What are the biggest payroll liabilities for a 12-person home care agency mixing W-2 and 1099?

### Takeaway
The biggest exposure is paying caregivers on 1099 when they do the same work as the W-2 staff. Agencies set schedules, assign clients and bill for the work, so under federal and state tests those caregivers are almost always employees. Reclassifying them brings back wages, liquidated damages (which double the back wages), back payroll taxes, and unpaid workers' comp and unemployment premiums. The next biggest risks are unpaid overtime and travel time, and in NY, Wage Parity violations.

### Cited Findings
- **Misclassification, federal.** DOL treats a worker as an employee under the FLSA based on economic dependence (the "economic reality" test). Misclassifying employees as independent contractors denies them minimum wage and overtime. — [DOL WHD](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification)
- **NELP:** home care workers are especially exposed to misclassification. Personal care work is seldom performed by genuinely independent businesses. — [NELP](https://www.nelp.org/insights-research/independent-contractor-classification-in-home-care/)
- **Case: Home Healthcare Corp.** The company and its owner paid $1.6M, split equally between back wages and liquidated damages, to 546 home health workers after a DOL misclassification claim. This shows the "double damages" pattern. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/home-healthcare-company-to-pay-16m-to-settle-dol-misclassification-claim/596440/)
- **Case: California.** The state reached a $10M settlement over misclassified home care workers. CA applies the ABC test (AB5), which caregivers almost never pass. — [Miller Shah](https://millershah.com/blog/california-home-care-worker-misclassification/)
- **Case: Ohio.** A home health company paid $975K to settle a collective action alleging program managers were misclassified as exempt (salaried) employees. Exempt-status mistakes also apply to office staff. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/topics/misclassification/wage-and-hour/home-health-care/)
- **Enforcement continues in 2026.** An April 2026 IC legal update lists home health among industries that "remain targets" for misclassification claims. — [JD Supra, Apr 2026](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/security-home-improvement-black-car-and-3517665/)
- **Case: WiCare Home Care Agency LLC.** The company was stuck with a $1.1M wage-case loss after the Sixth Circuit (Apr 2026) upheld DOL's authority to bar third-party employers from the companionship exemption. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/home-health-care-company-stuck-with-1-1-million-wage-case-loss); [6th Cir. opinion 26a0099p](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/26a0099p-06.pdf); [Parker Poe, Apr 2026](https://www.parkerpoe.com/news/2026/04/sixth-circuit-upholds-labor-departments-narrow-interpretation-of)
- **IRS and DOL can both pursue the same worker.** Settling with the IRS does not stop a DOL claim. — [Datastub blog, vendor source, low weight](https://www.datastub.us/blog/protect-home-care-agency-misclassification/)
- [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge] The IRS uses the common-law control test (behavioral, financial, relationship). IRC §3509 sets reduced rates for unintentional misclassification. The Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (VCSP, Form 8952) settles prospectively for about 10% of one year's employment tax liability. Section 530 relief does not apply where similar workers were treated as W-2. That is exactly the mixed W-2/1099 case, so a mixed agency loses its main federal safe harbor.
- [UNVERIFIED] **State tests:**
  - NJ uses the ABC test and has stop-work orders and misclassification penalties (2019-2021 laws).
  - NY: the DOL Unemployment Insurance Division audits are aggressive, and the Wage Theft Prevention Act adds damages.
  - PA has an active Joint Task Force on Misclassification.
  - FL uses common-law tests. However, FL nurse registries can legally place caregivers as independent contractors under Fla. Stat. 400.506. That is a real FL nuance; check it if a prospect is a registry and not an agency.
  - CA uses AB5's ABC test, with penalties of $5K-$25K per willful violation under Labor Code 226.8.

## Current status of the DOL third-party companionship exemption rule

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026, the 2013 Home Care Rule is still the law on the books. Third-party agencies technically cannot claim the companionship or live-in exemptions. However:
- DOL proposed restoring the exemptions on July 2, 2025.
- DOL stopped federal enforcement on July 25, 2025.
- A final rule (RIN 1235-AA51) was still pending as of the latest sources found (May-Aug 2026).

Private lawsuits and state laws still require minimum wage and overtime regardless. Do not tell prospects overtime "went away."

### Cited Findings
- **NPRM published 07/02/2025** (90 FR 28976). It proposes to let third-party employers, including home care staffing agencies, claim the §13(a)(15) companionship exemption and the §13(b)(21) live-in exemption, and to remove the 2013 limits on "provision of care." — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/02/2025-12316/application-of-the-fair-labor-standards-act-to-domestic-service)
- **Comments closed** Sept 2-3, 2025. — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/dol-proposes-rule-reinstate-companionship-live-exemptions-minimum-wage-and); [FR agenda](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16599/agenda-of-regulations)
- **Federal enforcement paused.** Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-4 (07/25/2025) told investigators to stop enforcing the 2013 rule against third-party agencies that claim the exemptions. — [Littler update](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/dol-proposes-rule-reinstate-companionship-live-exemptions-minimum-wage-and); [Goldberg Segalla](https://www.goldbergsegalla.com/news-and-knowledge/knowledge/significant-wage-requirement-changes-could-be-coming-for-providers-of-companion-services/)
- **No final rule yet.** Littler: "None of these proposed changes became final."
  - The DOL Spring 2026 regulatory agenda (FR, 08/14/2026) withdrew a duplicate entry, RIN 1235-AA55, on 12/15/2025.
  - The active rulemaking continues under RIN 1235-AA51, "Application of the FLSA to Domestic Service" (Reg Plan Seq. 94). I could not extract its projected final-rule date.
  - — [FR agenda 2026-16599](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16599/agenda-of-regulations)
- **Still pending around May 2026.** McKnight's reported that the industry "awaits the release of a major final rule." It also noted that the Sixth Circuit's WiCare decision supports DOL's authority to set the rule either way. — [McKnight's Home Care](https://www.mcknightshomecare.com/news/sixth-circuit-decision-on-overtime-case-backs-pending-companionship-exemption-rule/)
- **Context:** NPR (01/29/2026) reported the rescission was still a proposal. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/29/nx-s1-5626767/home-care-seniors-trump-labor-overtime)
- **Industry positions:** HCAOA and SBA Advocacy support the rescission. — [HCAOA](https://www.hcaoa.org/newsletters/reminder-take-action-on-companionship-exemption-today); [SBA Advocacy, 09/04/2025](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2025/09/04/advocacy-supports-dols-rescission-of-companion-care-rule/)
- **Where federal relief would not matter:**
  - The FAB does not stop private FLSA suits, and the 2013 regulation remains valid (WiCare, 6th Cir. 2026).
  - [UNVERIFIED] Many states independently require overtime for home care workers, including NY, NJ, CA (with its own IHSS/domestic worker rules), PA and IL. An agency that drops overtime on the strength of the federal pause can still be liable under state law.
- **Gap:** I could not confirm whether a final rule was published between Aug and Oct 2026. Re-check federalregister.gov for RIN 1235-AA51 before quoting.

## What payroll features mitigate these risks?

### Takeaway
The highest-value controls are:
- Moving everyone to W-2 with onboarding that handles state forms.
- Calculating overtime across multiple clients and job sites, including travel time.
- Integrating with EVV and scheduling so paid hours match visit data.
- Tracking NY Wage Parity and Medicaid pass-through spending at the pay-code level.
- Bundled workers' comp (pay-as-you-go) and unemployment insurance.

### Cited Findings — wage-hour pitfalls
- [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge; standard DOL guidance, see 29 CFR 785.38 and WHD Fact Sheet 79D]
  - **Travel time.** Travel between clients during the workday is compensable. Home-to-first-client commuting generally is not.
  - **Overtime across clients.** Overtime is owed when hours across all clients exceed 40 in the workweek.
  - **Per-visit pay.** Per-visit or flat pay must still meet minimum wage for all hours worked, including travel, charting and training. Overtime must be computed on the regular rate, which per-visit pay complicates.
  - **Live-in workers.** Sleep time can be excluded only with an agreement, at least 5 hours of uninterrupted sleep, and private quarters. NY's 13-hour live-in rule was upheld in *Andryeyeva* (NY Ct. App. 2019).
  - **Split shifts and spread of hours.** NY spread-of-hours pay is an extra hour at minimum wage when the workday spans more than 10 hours.
- **Exempt-status mistakes.** The $975K Ohio settlement shows that misclassifying coordinators and managers as exempt is also a liability. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/topics/misclassification/wage-and-hour/home-health-care/)

### Cited Findings — Medicaid pass-through and NY wage laws
- **NY Home Care Worker Minimum Wage (PHL 3614-f).**
  - NYC, Long Island and Westchester: $19.10 from 01/01/2025, rising to $19.65 from 01/01/2026. — [Rivkin Radler](https://www.rivkinradler.com/publications/ny-amends-home-care-worker-minimum-wage/)
  - [Conflicting/unclear] One alert lists $18.65 for 01/01/2026 in another region. That is likely the rest-of-state rate; confirm on dol.ny.gov. — [Hodgson Russ](https://www.hodgsonruss.com/newsroom/alerts/New-York-State-Increases-Minimum-Wage-and-Modifies-Other-Salary-Requirements)
- **NY Wage Parity (PHL 3614-c).**
  - Covers Medicaid-reimbursed home care in NYC, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester.
  - Requires minimum total compensation, made up of base wage plus a supplemental benefit.
  - The benefit portion is set at $3.22/hr in Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester. Statute text also references $4.09 for NYC.
  - — [NY DOL](https://dol.ny.gov/home-health-care-aides-and-wage-parity); [NY PHL 3614-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBH/3614-C)
  - [UNVERIFIED] Agencies must file annual certifications and, since 2022 amendments, annual independent audits to the DOL. Penalties include repayment and possible exclusion from Medicaid. This makes it a strong payroll-reporting selling point.
- **Federal 80/20 rule (CMS-2442-F, finalized April 2024).** States must ensure at least 80% of Medicaid payments for homemaker, home health aide and personal care services go to direct-care worker compensation. Effective July 2030. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/payment-rates-for-medicaid-home-care-ahead-of-the-2025-reconciliation-law/); [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/cms-finalizes-medicaid-access-rule-with-80-20-wage-pass-through/)
  - CMS is expected to propose revising or eliminating 80/20, and it has delayed other Access Rule provisions. Its status is uncertain. Do not sell against it as a fixed deadline. — [Paradigm](https://www.paradigmseniors.com/blog/cms-signals-potential-reversal-of-medicaids-80-20-rule-what-home-care-providers-should-know); [McKnight's](https://www.mcknightshomecare.com/news/cms-to-delay-enforcement-of-medicaid-access-rules-ipag-provision/)
- **State base-wage and pass-through examples:**
  - Colorado requires a $17.00/hr base wage for HCBS direct care workers. This continues as of 01/01/2026. — [CO HCPF](https://hcpf.colorado.gov/direct-care-workforce-base-wage)
  - Michigan: PA 22 of 2025 extended the direct care wage increase as a separate pass-through. — [MDHHS](https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs/-/media/Project/Websites/mdhhs/Assistance-Programs/Medicaid-BPHASA/Forms/MDHHS-LTC-DCW---SNF-FAQs-101525.pdf)
  - NGA notes states commonly require 80% of rate increases to be passed through, with records kept for audit. — [NGA](https://www.nga.org/publications/addressing-wages-of-the-direct-care-workforce-through-medicaid-policies/)
  - [UNVERIFIED] NJ, IL and MN have similar pass-through or percentage requirements.
- **EVV.** [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge] The 21st Century Cures Act §12006 requires electronic visit verification for Medicaid personal care services, in effect since 2020, and for home health services, with a deadline of 01/01/2024. Medicaid can cut federal matching funds to states that do not comply. EVV records each visit's type, date, location, worker, client and start and end times. Mismatches between EVV and payroll hours create both billing and wage-hour audit risk.

### Cited Findings — workers' comp and unemployment insurance
- [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]
  - Workers' comp is mandatory for employees in all target states. Texas is the only state where it is optional.
  - Home care has high injury rates from patient lifting, so class-code premiums are significant.
  - Misclassified 1099 caregivers lead to premium audits that charge back premium for uncovered payroll. In NY and NJ, they also bring penalties for failing to carry coverage.
  - Unemployment claims by a single "1099" caregiver commonly trigger state unemployment audits that reclassify all similar workers. This is the most common way small agencies get caught.

### Feature list for the pitch (mapped to the risks above)
1. W-2 onboarding with state new-hire and I-9 workflows, so reclassification is clean.
2. Weekly overtime calculated across all clients and locations, with weighted regular-rate math for per-visit or differential pay.
3. Travel-time and mileage pay codes, plus time import from EVV and scheduling tools (for example HHAeXchange, AxisCare, WellSky). These are vendor examples only; confirm integrations before claiming them.
4. NY Wage Parity pay codes: base, supplemental benefits and wage-parity reporting.
5. Pay-as-you-go workers' comp and unemployment insurance handling.
6. Audit-ready records and state minimum-wage rate tables.
